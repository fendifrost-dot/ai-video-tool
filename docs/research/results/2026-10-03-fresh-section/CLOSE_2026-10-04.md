## The three things, settled

### 1 · Shot 40's defect has a verdict, and acceptance covers what it was missing

The first acceptance model held a clip to timing, framing, lip sync, lighting and camera. The sneaker with no leg in
it is none of those, so shot 40 read "not yet verified" while two reports already described the defect.

- **Two more lines on every generated clip:** *Bodies and objects* (anatomy and object integrity — nothing missing,
  extra, detached or deformed) and *Action* (what happens is what was asked to happen). Nothing measures either;
  they are open until someone looks.
- **A second opinion's findings now count.** The Astra review is stored with each finding tied to a shot. A finding it
  called a blocker or major is a line of its own on the clip — source **reviewed**, apart from *measured* and
  *by eye* — and fails the clip until someone looks and overrules it. A finding belongs to the clip the shot was
  showing **when the review looked**: the review of 3 October saw the first shot-39 clip, so its "full length, not
  thighs-up" finding sits on that clip and not on the retest made five hours later. Reviews from now on record which
  clip they saw; an older review is tied to a clip only when exactly one generated clip was on the shot at the time,
  and otherwise to nothing.
- **Shot 40, judged:** *Bodies and objects* — **fails**: at the opening one white sneaker hangs tilted in the air
  with nothing in it, beside a second, planted shoe that has a leg; a trouser leg appears in it only near the end.
  *Action* — **fails**: asked for sneakers stepping onto the runway; in the five frames of the strip one shoe stays
  planted and the other hangs and drifts; no step lands with weight (read from stills, not watched in motion).
  Both are my reading, attributed in their notes, and Fendi's to take back. Astra's finding of 3 October says the
  same and now shows on the clip as *reviewed*.

A defect found live while doing this: a picture job's picture was being counted as "a generated clip" on its shot,
so shot 40 (one clip and the still it was made from) looked like two clips and the review's finding could be tied to

### 2 · A detected change is not the asked event

Corrected in three places.

- **The check's own words.** A beat now reads "a change, on time" / "a change, not on time", and its line says "a
  change of light begins at 3.67 s (−1.02 s)". Never "the change".
- **Acceptance.** *No change near the ask*, or *the only change near it is off its time*, fails timing without
  anyone looking — whatever that change is, nothing changed when it was asked to. *A change on time* no longer meets
  timing by itself: the line reads **cannot tell** ("whether it is the change that was asked for is not something the
  light can say") until someone looks at the frames and says it is — or says it is something else, which fails it.
  So a number can fail a clip; it cannot pass one.
- **The evidence shown before a restage.** It read "the change asked for was drawn and began 1.0 s early on average".
  It now reads: "a change of light began 1.0 s early on average (−1.02 s, −0.94 s). That it was the change asked for
  was read off the frames by eye, not measured." The record carries `identified: "by_eye"`.

What supports the identification for the two automatic numbers: the frames before, at and after each (in
`RESULTS.md`) — the room going dark with points of light on `c038`, the mirror ball coming alive on the first `c035`.
That is a person's reading. It is evidence; it is not a measurement, and the record now says which is which.

### 3 · The $2.22 has a defined scope — and a rule behind it

It is not a discount and not an empirical rate. It is Higgsfield's published pricing rule for the model, which AVT's
estimate had half-applied.

**The rule** (open.higgsfield.ai → Seedance 2.5 → Reference to Video → "Price", read 4 October):

> billable video tokens = ceil(output height × output width × (input video duration + generated video duration) × 24 / 1024)
> — $0.0214 per 1,000 tokens at 480p or 720p, $0.0234 at 1080p; **if video inputs are provided the token rate is
> multiplied by 0.6**; both input and generated durations are billable; image and audio references do not count as
> video input; rates are before any customer discount.

AVT's old estimate billed input and output seconds at the full rate (list × 2 = $3.70). The rule bills both at six
tenths: 720 × 1280 × (4.004 + 4) s × 24 / 1024 = 172,887 tokens × $0.01284 per thousand = **$2.22**. For six
seconds: **$3.33**. Both are the amounts charged.

**What the five charges were, exactly** (from AVT's stored requests and the request builder):

| | |
|---|---|
| Model / mode | `seedance-2.5-reference`, `reference_to_video` |
| Resolution / aspect | 720p, 9:16 — the files that came back are 720 × 1280 (read from each file by Check this cut) |
| Generated duration | 4 s asked (four jobs), 6 s (one); the files are 4.04 s and 6.04 s, and the charge matches the seconds asked |
| Video input | one — the cut of the take, as long as the output (4.004 s, 6.006 s) |
| Image input | one (the place picture) — not billed as video |
| Audio | `generate_audio: false` |
| Discount | none: the account's "discounts" line reads −$0.00; the advertised 30 % applies only after a $200 or $1,000 top-up |

**What the estimate does now.** `seedanceUsd(resolution, outputSeconds, inputSeconds)` is the rule itself
(`config/provider_rates.json` → `seedance_tokens`, with the rule and this scope written beside the numbers). So:

| 4 s restage, source as long as the output | Estimate | Standing |
|---|---|---|
| 720p | $2.22 | **charged** (five times) |
| 480p | $0.99 | published rule; not yet charged |
| 1080p | $5.46 | published rule; not yet charged |
| 720p from a 10 s source | $3.88 | published rule; not yet charged — a longer source is paid for |

Not covered by anything observed: more than one video input, audio on, a discount. A discount would lower the
charge; it is not modelled.

## The subscription decision

ChatGPT's table is right, and it follows from the corrected price: Plus monthly is $59 for 1,200 credits; a 4 s 720p
restage is 28 credits, so 42 a month; the same 42 on the API are $93.24; the plan saves $34.24 (37 %) **only at full
use**, and breaks even at 27 restages a month. With restaging paused and no clip accepted, there is no volume to
justify it. **Not bought. The connectors stay connected; routing stays off (PR #167).** The same rule changes the
other sizes the integration agent quoted: 480p is 12 credits ($0.59 at full use) against $0.99 on the API; 1080p is
48 credits ($2.36) against $5.46.

## Next: keep the performance, change the environment

Not started. What is known, so the test can be small and exact.

**It has been done once already, on existing footage.** `bar6` (2 October, scripts lane): 43.3 s of bars 24–46 in
which twelve of fifteen performance cuts are his **real take** — matted, placed in a generated place, with a
2.5D camera move — and three are Seedance angles. It passed the coverage check (89 % of cuts moving). It was never
held to today's acceptance lines, and Fendi has not ruled on the one thing that decides this route. By construction

**What decides it is integration, not fidelity.** The earlier finding (`2026-10-02-world-around-performer`): a take
pasted over a plate "reads as a cutout — closet light on him". The answers built since are a grade match to the
plate, a foreground occluder (hide his waist cut behind something that stays), placement and depth. Whether those are
enough in a dark, hard-lit place like the runway is the open question, and it is a question for the eye.

**The test.** Shots 39–42 again — same lyric, same place entity (`PARIS_BLACK_RUNWAY`, picture already approved and
paid for), same beat at 1.9 s — with the take kept:

1. matte the take over 2:36.86–2:54.51; place it in the runway picture (or a living plate made from it);
2. the light change at the beat is the edit's own effect on the song clock — exact by construction, already in the
   product (rev 59);

   timing against the effect; lighting, integration, bodies and objects by eye; Astra as a second opinion.

**Cost.** $0 with the still as the place; up to $1.05 if three shots get a living plate (Kling, $0.35 each).
