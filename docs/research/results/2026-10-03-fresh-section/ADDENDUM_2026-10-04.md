## 2 · The measurement defect, corrected

**What was wrong.** The retest clip opens dark, comes up by 0.63 s, and lights the mirror ball at 0.96 s. The beat
check (v3) read that as ONE change of light beginning at 0.50 s, paired the beat with it, and reported
**"displaced, −1.40 s"** — the timing of the clip's unasked opening, presented as the timing of the mirror ball. The
retest report said so in prose; the record on the clip, and anything reading it, did not.

**What it does now (`beatCheck.ts`, v4).** A beat is timed only when there is no choice to make. When there are more
changes of light near the beats than beats — a change made in steps, with the light standing between them, counting
as its steps — the verdict is **`undetermined`**: the changes that could be the one asked for are listed, and there
is no number. Live on the retest clip:

> 1.13 s, brighter) and one change was asked for. Which of them is the one asked for cannot be told from the light,
> so no timing is given: the frames are the check

with the four frames beside it (before, each change as it has arrived, where it was asked).

## 4 · Failures now affect acceptance

`src/lib/storyboard/acceptance.ts`. Five things are asked of a generated clip — **timing, framing, lip sync, lighting,
camera and movement** — and each has one finding: **meets · fails · cannot tell · not verified**, with where it comes
from beside it: **measured** (read off the file) or **by eye** (decided by someone looking, kept with a note and a
date). A judgement by eye settles its line and never rewrites the measurement, which stays shown as measured. A clip
is accepted only when every line is met; one failure fails it; anything open leaves it *not yet verified*, which is
not a pass.

Where it shows: on the shot (a chip on the clip and the panel under the two checks), in Review (a chip on each shot,
a line in Checks, and a card "What was asked for"), and beside **Check this cut**, which now says in so many words
that it is about whether the cut plays.
