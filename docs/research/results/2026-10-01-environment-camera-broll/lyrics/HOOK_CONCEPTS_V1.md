## H1 — “You don't gotta cut the lights on / This ice on”

- **Frost does the switch** (literal, realism risk medium) — Nobody touches a switch. A thin clear glaze of ice creeps over the already-wet black floor and the lower panels until the existing white light strips ricochet so hard the corridor reads fully lit. *Camera:* slow low-angle 35mm push-in along the floor. *Motion/FX:* frost bloom spreading toward camera over eight seconds, slow flare on the strips, faint vapor, no shatter *Why:* The lyric becomes a physical fact: the ice itself is the light, so the lights never have to come on. *Risk:* a spreading ice glaze on a wet floor can read CG if the growth is not kept slow and simple
- **The corridor ices itself** (surreal, realism risk high) — The vertical white light strips thicken into slow-growing ice columns that glow from inside; each of his slow steps sends a silent white crack skating ahead through the black floor like frozen lightning. *Camera:* lateral 50mm track, slight dutch as the cracks race. *Motion/FX:* slow inward growth of ice on the strips, single-file light-cracks in the floor, no debris, no fast shatter *Why:* The boast leaves his body and takes the architecture: the whole hall becomes the ice, so light is already on. *Risk:* transforming light strips into growing ice and drawing light-filled cracks is complex physics for a still-to-video pass
- **He arrives, the lights answer** (performance, realism risk low) — He stands centered in the corridor, chin slightly lifted, unmoving. The camera finds him and the existing strips give one slow extra pulse of brightness, as if his presence threw the switch. *Camera:* locked medium, then a very slow dolly-in to chest-up. *Motion/FX:* one slow blink, faint breathing, a single delayed pulse on the white strips, reflections sliding on the wet floor *Why:* The performance is the claim: he does not cut the lights on; they come up because he is there. *Risk:* mostly a still portrait with a simple light pulse, well inside image-to-video limits

- **Gold Plates Won't Stick** (surreal, realism risk high) — Blank gold plates try to tag his cream jacket as he walks; they fail, slide off, and drop onto the wet floor with small slow splashes. He never looks down. He keeps walking into a hard white shaft of light that opens like a tailor’s cut. *Camera:* 40mm lateral track, slight push as the light shaft opens. *Motion/FX:* A few blank gold plates slide down the knit and fall; tiny slow splashes on black water; a hard white shaft widens ahead of him; haze in the crushed blacks. *Why:* Brands want to adhere to him and cannot; he follows the cut itself — the designer’s act, not the label. *Risk:* Rigid plates sliding off fabric plus liquid contact often tear, stick, or pop in image-to-video.
